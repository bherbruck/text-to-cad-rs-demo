#Kernel benchmark: OCCT, solidcore and cadrs

An independent head-to-head of the three kernels behind --kernel. All three build the same model IR, and each result is checked against references that no kernel computed. The adapters were run as they are: nothing was changed or tuned for this benchmark. The test-port agent wrote the harness and this report. It did not write either adapter.

Bottom line. Ship cadrs as the alternative to OCCT, not solidcore. It is the fastest of the three: in native builds it takes 0.55× OCCT's time (geometric mean) and runs the scaling cases 10–100× faster. It returned no silent wrong answers. Its build rate matches solidcore's, and 16 of its 34 failures are one adapter policy that is easy to fix. solidcore is just as accurate when it builds, but it is 16.5× slower than OCCT, timed out on two of the scaling cases, and made three twisted lofts without an error. OCCT stays the default. It built the most cases (243 of 271), but it is not infallible: it returned 2 silent wrong answers that both other kernels and the reference got right.

Reproduce with scripts/bench-kernels.sh. It writes everything to target/bench/: the corpus, results.jsonl, report.md, summary.json with every verdict, and wasm.jsonl. The harness lives in crates/kernel-bench (one / drive / report), scripts/bench/corpus.ts, scripts/bench/wasm.mjs and scripts/bench/wasm-report.mjs.

#Method

  • Builds: native release builds (cargo build --release -p kernel-bench --features solidcore,cadrs,

    CARGO_TARGET_DIR=target/bench). The kernels are pinned as the workspace pins them: solidcore 4c938d2, cadrs eb5899e, and OCCT through occt-capi-sys.

  • Runs: each case on each kernel is a fresh process, run 3 times. Timing runs happen

    only when the first run built. Time is the median in-process build time (Engine::build: IR → B-rep, cold cache). Process wall time is reported too. Every process is killed after 60 s, with an 8 GB address-space cap. Panics are caught, and crashes, timeouts and errors are all recorded.

  • Recorded per case and kernel:
    • build ok or error, with the message
    • volume, area and bounds
    • is_valid
    • part, solid and face counts
    • the kernel's own tessellation at the engine's default quality: its volume (signed tetrahedra),

      and its open edges after welding the vertices

    • the interference report, for documents with 2–40 solid parts
  • Correctness reference: first a volume no kernel computed, where one exists (159 of 271

    cases), otherwise OCCT. The neutral references are:

    • Analytic:
      • box fillet/chamfer on |Z
      • box shells
      • tapered extrudes (prismatoid / frustum)
      • revolves (Pappus)
      • lofts between like sections (frustum)
      • the N-hole plate
    • Exact: the union of N boxes, by coordinate compression.
    • Monte Carlo over the generator's own CSG tree: 2 000 000 Halton points per case, with a

      binomial error bar. The 13 csg shells use a Monte Carlo over signed distances: the solid minus its inward erosion. The tolerance there is 2 %, because a kernel may join at sharp corners where the erosion rounds them.

  • Silent wrong answer: the build succeeded, but the result is invalid, or its volume is off

    the reference by more than 1 % (or 3σ of a Monte Carlo reference, if larger). Where there is no neutral reference, more than 1 % off OCCT counts. Lofts between unlike sections (hexagon ↔ rectangle) are flagged as ill-defined: their surface depends on how a kernel pairs vertices. They are judged separately below, against the two natural, untwisted pairings.

  • Machine: Minisforum box, Linux 7.0, shared with other agents. The timings are therefore

    indicative: ratios are taken per case and then averaged geometrically, which cancels most of the background load.

#Agreement

When a kernel builds, it is almost always right. Against the 159 neutral references:

Against OCCT, on the cases both built:

solidcore22121721922022115510 / 10
cadrs22121922122022116110 / 10
  • Face counts often differ. This is topology bookkeeping: whether a cylinder or sphere carries

    a seam, and whether coplanar faces are merged. It is not a geometric difference, but it means face indices in references are kernel-specific. Stable refs (~fp) exist for this reason.

  • is_valid returned true for every built result on every kernel, so it caught nothing here.

    The volume checks did.

  • Mesh self-consistency (tessellation volume against the kernel's own exact volume, 2 %) and

    open edges flagged 6 results:

    • OCCT: ex-spring-spring 7.2 % and csg-030-d6 2.1 %, and a tessellation failure on

      csg-069-d5.

    • solidcore: ex-spring-spring 2.8 % and csg-055-d3 2.4 %.
    • cadrs: 3 open edges on csg-084-d4.

    The spring is a helical sweep, where the default mesh quality is coarse for all three kernels.

#Failures

All three failed on 10 cases, which are mostly generator over-reach:

  • 4 chamfers and 1 fillet whose selector matched no edges: identical errors from all three.
  • 2 over-sized fillets and 1 over-sized shell.
  • csg-090-d8-snap and csg-108-d7-snap, where an intermediate common is empty (see below).

OCCT failed on 28. What the other two did with them:

Failures specific to solidcore (OCCT built the case):

  • 16 cases where an intermediate common is genuinely empty. A Monte Carlo check of the sub-tree

    finds 0 points in it. OCCT carries the empty result through to a correct final volume. solidcore's adapter refuses: boolean failed: the result is empty (crates/cad-kernel-solidcore/src/lib.rs:996).

  • 2 timeouts over 60 s: scale-plate-holes-500 and scale-union-boxes-200.
  • part-nema23: boolean failed: the result is not a closed solid.
  • ex-spring-duct (a shell): not a closed solid.
  • feat-021-shell-csg, the case OCCT got wrong: not a manifold solid.
  • feat-005-fillet-csg: a fillet radius of 2.04 mm on a 30 mm edge was refused with "status 1001".

Failures specific to cadrs (OCCT built the case):

  • The same 16 empty-intermediate common cases, from the same adapter policy

    (crates/cad-kernel-cadrs/src/lib.rs:1210).

  • 4 booleans: csg-062-d7 and csg-099-d4-snap left an open shell, csg-069-d5 used an edge on

    3 faces, and csg-114-d3 produced a self-crossing loop.

  • ex-spring-duct: shell, open shell.
  • feat-019-fillet-csg: "only edges between two faces can be blended".

Once the empty-intermediate policy is fixed, the kernel-specific failures where OCCT succeeded drop to 6 for cadrs and 6 for solidcore (4 errors plus 2 timeouts).

#Conclusion

  1. Alternative kernel: cadrs.
    • Correctness: on everything it built it matched the neutral references (median error 0.0087 %),

      with zero silent wrong answers. It was right in both cases where OCCT was wrong.

    • Speed: it is the fastest kernel in every family, natively and in the browser.
    • Its weak spots: 4 boolean failures on random chains, one fillet, one shell, and the

      empty-intermediate policy it shares with solidcore. It fails loudly rather than wrongly, which is the failure mode to want.

  2. solidcore is not ready to be the alternative.
    • It is as accurate as the others on booleans and features when it builds.
    • But it is 16.5× slower than OCCT and about 30× slower than cadrs (geometric mean). It times

      out on scaling cases that the others build in 0.2–2 s.

    • Its lofts between unlike sections twist silently, 6–25 % off any untwisted surface.
    • It refuses 5 shells that cadrs builds correctly.
  3. OCCT stays the default, with the most builds (243). It is the only kernel that carries

    empty intermediates through, and it builds the fillet/shell edge cases the others refuse. It is not infallible:

    • one silent 35 % shell error
    • one 1.8 % volume-integration error
    • 8 needless refusals of r1 = r2 cones
    • 7 boolean failures on coincident faces, which the others handled.

    For DFM/FEA, where volume matters, a cheap guard is worth considering: cross-check the tessellation volume against the exact one. It would have caught csg-030-d6.

  4. Cheap fixes this run found. None of them was applied here, as the benchmark reports as-is:
    • In both adapters, let an empty boolean result flow on as an empty body, the way OCCT does.

      That would recover 16 cases each.

    • In occt-rs, accept a cone with r1 = r2 (a cylinder): 8 cases.
    • In solidcore, look at the loft vertex pairing between unlike sections.
