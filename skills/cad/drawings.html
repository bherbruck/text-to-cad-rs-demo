#Engineering drawings

Read this when the person asks for a drawing, a dimensioned sheet, shop or manufacturing drawings, a blueprint, or "2D views of this part". A drawing is projected from the model's geometry, so it follows the model: change the model, run it again, export the drawing again.

A drawing is not a DXF cut file: for a laser/waterjet profile or a flat pattern, export .dxf (exports).

#Make one

cad run src/bracket.ts                       # build; note the id it prints
cad model export "$ID" drawings/bracket.pdf --part-number BR-001 --general-tolerance "ISO 2768-m"

.pdf and .svg both write the drawing: one landscape A-size sheet (A3 unless --sheet) with

  • the top, front and right views in third-angle projection (--projection first for first

    angle) and an isometric in the free corner (--views picks: front top right left bottom back iso);

  • visible edges heavy, hidden edges dashed and grey (tangent edges behind material are left out;

    --no-hidden drops hidden lines), centre lines on every circle seen end-on;

  • overall dimensions, each model axis once (width and height on the front view, depth on the top);
  • hole callouts from the geometry, grouped by size and drilling axis: 4× Ø6 THRU,

    Ø4 × 5 DEEP (--no-dimensions drops dimensions and callouts);

  • a title block: title (the model name unless --title), part number, material (the parts'

    materials unless --material), scale, units (mm), date (today unless --date), revision, and the projection symbol; numbered notes above it (--general-tolerance is note 1, then each --note).

The scale is the largest standard one (50:1, 20:1, 10:1, 5:1, 2:1, 1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 1:10, …) at which the views fit the sheet. --scale 0.5 asks for 1:2; if that does not fit, the export is refused and names a scale that does (or says to use a larger --sheet).

#Check it

Look at the drawing before handing it over. pdftoppm -png -r 80 bracket.pdf out (or open the SVG) and read it:

  • every hole has a centre mark and its callout says the right size and count;
  • the overall dimensions are the part's size (they are measured from the geometry; if one looks

    wrong the model is wrong);

  • hidden lines appear where features are behind faces.

#What it does not do

  • Only overall sizes and holes are dimensioned automatically: feature positions (hole pitch, slot

    widths) are not. Say so in the hand-off, or add a note (--note "HOLES ON 28 × 18 PITCH.").

  • No sections, detail or auxiliary views; tolerances are only the ones given

    (--general-tolerance, notes), never invented.

  • Counterbores and countersinks are called out as their separate diameters and depths.
  • Threads are not recognised: a modelled tapped hole is called out at its modelled diameter.
