#Selectors

Read this file when picking the edges to fillet or chamfer, or the faces to open with shell.

#Contents

  • Strings
  • Builders
  • Indices
  • Check what it matched

A selector is evaluated by the server on the shape it is called on, after that shape is built: so it always sees the edges the last operation made. The same selector selects faces for shell and edges for fillet / chamfer.

#Strings

CadQuery-style:

StringEdgesFaces
>ZThose whose centre is farthest along +Z (a box's 4 top edges)The farthest (a box's top)
<ZFarthest along -ZThe bottom
|ZStraight edges along Z (a box's 4 vertical edges)Planar faces with normal ±Z (top and bottom)
#ZStraight edges ⟂ Z (the 8 horizontal edges)Planar faces whose normal ⟂ Z (the 4 sides)
%LINE %CIRCLE %ELLIPSE %SPLINEBy curve type–
%PLANE %CYLINDER %CONE %SPHERE %TORUS–By surface type
*AllAll
  • Axes: X, Y, Z, a negative one (>-Z is the same as <Z), or a vector: >(1, 1, 0).
  • Combine with and, or, not and parentheses. not binds tightest, then and, then or.
  • >Z and <Z compare centres, with a small tolerance, and match any type: on a filleted top,

    >Z faces is the flat top; on a cylinder, >Z edges is the top circle.

box(40, 30, 10).fillet("|Z", 3)                     // the 4 vertical edges
box(40, 30, 10).fillet("|Z and >X", 3)              // the 2 vertical edges at +X
box(40, 30, 10).chamfer(">Z", 1)                    // the 4 top edges
plate.chamfer("%CIRCLE and >Z", 0.5)                // hole rims on the top face
box(40, 30, 10).shell(">Z", -2)                     // open the top face
tube.shell(">Z or <Z", -1.5)                        // open both ends
part.fillet("%CIRCLE and not >Z", 0.5)             // circular edges, except those on the top

Not supported (the parser says so): indexed selectors like >Z[1], the +Z/-Z normal filters and exc. Use and not, near or inside instead.

#Builders

For what strings can't say, sel builds the same JSON, chained with .and(), .or(), .not() (which also take strings):

BuilderSelects
sel.max("Z"), sel.min("X")As >Z, <X
sel.parallel("Z"), sel.perpendicular("Z")As |Z, #Z
sel.type("circle")As %CIRCLE
sel.near([x, y, z], count = 1)The count items whose centres are nearest a point
sel.inside([x0, y0, z0], [x1, y1, z1])Items whose centres lie inside a box
sel.radius(min, max = min)Circular edges / cylindrical faces with radius in range
sel.index(3, 7)By the kernel's numbers
sel.all()Everything
import { sel } from "@cad/sdk";

body.fillet(sel.near([0, 5, 5]), 4)                              // the one edge at an inside bend
gear.chamfer(sel.radius(5).and("%CIRCLE", ">Z or <Z"), 0.6)      // both rims of a 5 mm bore
body.fillet(sel.inside([-50, -50, 9], [50, 50, 11]).and("#Z"), 1) // horizontal edges near z = 10

near is the precise tool when you know where the edge is: you designed the geometry, so you know the centre of the edge you want (an edge's centre is its midpoint).

#Indices

[3, 7] (or sel.index(3, 7)) selects by the kernel's face or edge numbers, as cad model inspect and the viewer show them. Numbers change whenever the topology does, so use them only for a one-off or when the person picked the element in the viewer; prefer a geometric selector that says why.

#Check what it matched

A fillet that fails, or rounds the wrong edges, usually means the selector matched something other than intended.

  1. await m.ir() (or reading the parse error) shows what a string parsed to.
  2. Build the shape before the fillet and cad model inspect "$ID" "$ID#<part>/edge N" the edges you

    expect: their center/mid, direction and curve say whether >Z, |Z or near will catch them.

  3. A snapshot with --display wireframe shows where the edges are.
