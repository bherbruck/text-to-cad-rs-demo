#CNC milling

Read this file before modelling or checking a machined part. What every process shares is the review reference.

#Design it

Figures from Protolabs Network's (Hubs) CNC design guide; cad's own floors where it gives none.

  • Every tool is round. An inside vertical corner always has a radius: at least the smallest

    tool's (half its diameter), and a third of the cavity's depth where it can be. A sharp inside vertical corner is an error; 1.25 mm or more is recommended (a 2.5 mm end mill).

  • Cavities no deeper than 4 times their width (30 at most, with special tooling).
  • Holes: standard drill sizes, 1 mm or more, no deeper than 4 diameters (40 at most).
  • Walls: 0.8 mm metal and 1.5 mm plastic recommended; 0.5 and 1.0 the least.
  • Access. Every face is reached by a straight tool from one of the machine's directions: on 3

    axes the six principal ones, one setup each. A face no direction reaches is an undercut (a T-slot or dovetail cutter, or more axes); a closed cavity cannot be cut.

  • No knife edges: blunt an acute convex edge.

#Check it

cad model dfm "$ID" --process cnc --material aluminum --tools 3,6,10
cad model dfm "$ID" --process cnc --axes 5
  • --tools: the shop's end mill diameters. With none, corners are judged against the smallest tool

    the guide calls feasible and recommended against a 2.5 mm end mill (cnc.tools_assumed).

  • --material: walls are judged by its class (metal or plastic); with none, as a metal.
  • --axes 3|4|5 (default 3): 4 adds the diagonals about X; 5 reaches from 26 directions.

#Read it

The families: internal_corners (sharp, radius_mm, height_mm, radius_to_depth), pockets (depth_mm, width_mm, depth_to_width), holes (depth_to_diameter, axis), walls, knife_edges, access (unreached_fraction per face), sharp_edges (inside edges no end mill's flank or end makes), voids, setups (setup_count, directions: the fewest directions that reach every face).

#Review the part

Check by hand: threads (sizes and lengths are not read), tolerances and surface finish, fixturing, the stock size, and a cavity's width taken as its floor's narrower side (a non-rectangular pocket's true width can be less).
