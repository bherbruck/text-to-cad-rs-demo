#FEA: will it hold, and how far does it move

Checks whether a part, or an assembly of bonded parts, is strong and stiff enough under its loads, as a linear static stress study (FEA) with gmsh and CalculiX. Use when the person asks whether a part will hold, break or bend too much, where it is most stressed, how much load it takes, how to make it stronger or lighter, or for FEA, a stress analysis or a strength simulation. Reports peak von Mises stress, safety factor against yield and deflection. Static loads only, not vibration, buckling, heat, fatigue or impact.

A study holds some faces of a model, loads others, and solves the linear elastic response: stress and displacement under forces, pressures, the parts' own weight and steady accelerations, with an isotropic material below yield, rigid fixtures, and loads that do not move. Parts of an assembly are bonded where they touch. It is a first-pass engineering check, not a certification: say so in the report. Vibration, buckling, heat, fatigue, impact and sliding contact are outside it.

Units are fixed: mm, N, MPa (pressure, modulus, stress), t/mm³ for density (steel 7.85e-9) and g (9.81 m/s²) for gravity and acceleration. Restate every load in them first.

The mesher (gmsh) and the solver (ccx, CalculiX) must be installed (apt install gmsh calculix-ccx); CAD_GMSH / CAD_CCX name other binaries.

#Workflow

  1. Build the model (cad run src/bracket.ts; note its id) with a material on each part

    (.label("bracket", { material: "aluminum" }): the SDK's presets, aluminum, steel, stainless, brass, titanium, petg, abs, nylon, ..., are names the study's table knows; aluminum reads as 6061), or give a grade for every part with --material or in the study.

  2. List the faces and pick the ones to hold and load:
    cad model fea "$ID"            # no study: prints every face with its ref, area, centre and a hint

    Hints read like plane, normal -Z, largest or cylinder, r 3. Match the person's words ("the mounting face", "the hole") by hint and centre. A face picked in the viewer arrives as a ref already (<id>#bracket/face 17). Face numbers move when the model changes: list again.

  3. Solve with flags for a simple study, or a study file for anything more:
    cad model fea "$ID" --fix 'bracket/face 4' --force 'bracket/face 9:0,0,-500' --material 'aluminium 6061' --json
cad model fea "$ID" --study study.json --snapshot stress.png --json

    The flags add to a study: --fix REF and --roller REF (repeat for several), --force 'REF:fx,fy,fz' (N), --pressure 'REF:MPa', --gravity [gx,gy,gz] (bare: 0,0,-1 g), --material NAME, --margin N (default 2), --size MM, --no-refine, --snapshot OUT.png.

    {
  "fixtures": [{"faces": ["bracket/face 4"], "type": "fixed"}],
  "loads": [
    {"faces": ["bracket/face 9"], "type": "force", "vector_N": [0, 0, -500]},
    {"faces": ["bracket/face 12"], "type": "pressure", "pressure_MPa": 0.2},
    {"type": "gravity", "vector_g": [0, 0, -1]}
  ],
  "material": "aluminium 6061",
  "margin": 2,
  "checks": [{"kind": "displacement", "limit_mm": 0.5, "faces": ["bracket/face 9"], "label": "Tip sag"}]
}
    • fixed holds a face still; roller lets an axis-facing plane slide in its plane (a symmetry

      cut, a frictionless support).

    • A force is the total over its faces, spread by area; a pressure pushes into the surface;

      gravity/acceleration act on the whole volume (acceleration loads the opposite way) and need a density.

    • material: a table name (steel, s355, stainless 304, aluminium 6061, 7075, brass,

      copper, titanium, PLA, PETG, ABS, nylon, POM, polycarbonate, …) or {"E_MPa", "nu", "yield_MPa", "density_t_per_mm3"}; parts gives one part its own. Left out, each part's model material is looked up (yields are the low end of each grade's range). Never pick a material to make a study run: ask.

    • mesh.size_mm defaults to a fortieth of the bounding diagonal; gmsh refines at curved

      features by itself. When the safety factor is under 3 the run solves again at half the size and reports both (refined); mesh.refine: false stops that.

  4. Read the result (--json prints it; exit status 1 when a finding is an error):

    summary, then measurements: max_von_mises_mpa and the face nearest it, safety_factor against yield_mpa (the weakest part's), max_displacement_mm and where, applied_force_n against reaction_force_n (they must balance), the mesh, and each part's numbers. Look at the snapshot (--snapshot stress.png, blue low → red at the peak) or the viewer's stress overlay.

  5. Report with units: the peak von Mises and the feature it is near, the safety factor and the

    yield it is against, the largest displacement and where, the load against the reaction, the mesh, whether a finer solve ran; then the findings and what the model assumes.

#Judge the answer

Estimate by hand first: beam bending for a cantilever (tip deflection PL³/3EI, root stress Mc/I), about three times the nominal stress beside a hole in tension, F/A in a bar, p·r/t in a thin ring. Within roughly 20 % the model does what you asked; off by a factor, check the load's direction and units, the faces, then the material. The suite checks a 100 × 10 × 10 steel cantilever against PL³/3EI + shear (0.191 vs 0.192 mm) and Mc/I (60 MPa at the root, 30 at mid-span).

#Findings

FindingSeverityMeansDo
yieldserrorThe nodal peak is above yieldStrengthen where the peak is, change the material, or confirm the load
displacement_over_limiterrorA displacement check is past its limitStiffen the part or confirm the limit
low_marginwarningIt holds, but under marginAs for yields, or report the margin it has; never lower margin to clear it
peak_at_fixturewarningThe peak sits on a held face's edgeA rigid fixture concentrates stress: read the stress a little away from it
peak_concentrationwarningThe integration-point peak is well above the nodal oneA sharp corner or point load the mesh has not resolved: fillet it or spread the load
mesh_not_convergedwarningThe finer solve moved the peak by more than 10 %A peak that keeps rising is a singularity; otherwise set a smaller mesh.size_mm
large_displacementwarningIt moves more than 1 % of its sizeThe small-displacement model is stretched
reaction_mismatchwarningThe reactions do not balance the loadSomething leaks: check the faces and the fixtures

A refused study (no fixture, a load on a held face, a face ref that is not a face, a part with no material, a part free to move) is an error naming the field, before or instead of solving.

#Assemblies

Parts that touch are fragmented into one conformal mesh, so they are bonded (glued) where they touch; each keeps its own material and is judged against its own yield (parts), and weakest_part names the one the safety factor is for. Every part must reach a fixture through bonded neighbours. Sliding, bolted or pressed joints are not modelled.
