#Renders for people

Renders photoreal pictures and turntables of a text-to-cad model - a hero shot, a product or README picture, a transparent cut-out, a posed mechanism, a frame of an animation, a turntable GIF. Use when the person wants a picture for people to look at. Not for checking geometry, which the cad skill's snapshot does.

A render is a picture for a person: a hero image, a README picture, a turntable. cad model render path-traces the document the cad server holds, in Rust, on every core: soft shadows, reflections, ambient occlusion, metals and plastics from each part's material, a studio or a sky, depth of field. Nothing else is installed. Geometry is judged with cad model snapshot, never a render: lens, light and depth of field make it a poor measure.

#Start with the task

TaskCommand
A still, quickcad model render "$ID" hero.png (a draft: 800×600, 16 samples, a second or two)
The finalcad model render "$ID" hero.png --preset final (1600×1200, 256 samples, tens of seconds)
A cut-out for a page--background transparent (the shadow stays, in alpha)
A flat colour behind--background "#f4efe6", or white
Another view, a longer lens--camera front, --camera 1,-2,0.5, --lens 85
A pose, a frame of an animation--pose open, --pose '{"lid": 45}', --animation showcase --time 1.5
A turntablecad model render "$ID" turn.gif --turntable --frames 36
A turntable playing an animation... turn.gif --turntable --animation showcase --animate
Frames to cut a film fromcad model render "$ID" frames/ --turntable --frames 120 (PNG sequence), then ffmpeg
One part alone--part gripper/finger_left

Draft first, look at it, then the final with the same options: the framing, light and pose are the same, only cleaner. Every render is deterministic: the same options and --seed give the same image.

#Looks come from the model

A part's look is its material and colour, set in the model (.label("lid", { color, material })), not in the render:

MaterialRenders as
aluminum, steel, stainless, titanium, brass, bronze, copper, gold, chromeMetal; a colour tints it (anodised aluminium: material: "aluminum", color: "#2255cc")
pla, petg, abs, asa, nylon, pom, pc …Plastic in the part's colour, a satin sheen
rubber, tpuMatte, near black unless coloured
oak, pine, plywood, mdfA matte wood tone
glass, uncoloured acrylic / pcClear, refracting
nonePlastic in the part's colour

#Light, ground and background

  • --environment studio (default): a light grey studio, a key softbox high on the left, a fill and

    a rim. sky: a blue sky and a sun, crisp shadows. white: even light, a clay look that shows form. dark: a key and a rim on near black. The lights turn with the camera, so every view is lit alike.

  • --ground shadow (default): the model stands on an invisible floor that only catches its shadow.

    plane shows a matte floor (--ground-color); none floats the model.

  • --exposure 0.5 brightens by half a stop; --intensity scales the lights.

#Depth of field

--fstop 8 focuses on the model's centre. Scene units are millimetres, so a hand-sized part through a 50 mm lens is a macro shot: f/2.8 blurs nearly all of it, f/8 to f/16 keeps a part sharp with a soft background. Without --fstop everything is sharp.

#In the browser

The playground renders in its worker with the same options (single-threaded: a 400×300 draft takes a few seconds), showing each pass as it refines, and saves the PNG or GIF.

#Check each change

ChangeCheck
Camera, framing, lightA draft, opened and looked at
A pose or an animation frameA draft at that pose: every part where the snapshot puts it
A turntableA few frames first (--frames 4), then the whole turn
The finalAgainst its draft: same framing, nothing missing, no speckle
  • A black or empty picture: the part filter matched nothing, or exposure is far off.
  • Speckle that does not go away: more --samples; glass and polished metal need the most.
  • A render is slow when it is big: time grows with pixels × samples × bounces. Draft at 800×600.
